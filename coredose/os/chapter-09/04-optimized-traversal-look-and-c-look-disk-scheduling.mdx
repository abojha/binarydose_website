### 💻 Bridging to Computer Science

In both **SCAN** and **C-SCAN**, the read/write head mechanically travels to the physical ends of the disk platter (Cylinder $0$ and Cylinder $199$). 

In real-world storage controllers, traveling to the physical end of a disk when no request is waiting there is pure wasted mechanical motion.

To optimize head motion, operating systems implement **LOOK** and **C-LOOK**:
* Before moving the actuator arm to the next cylinder, the controller **looks ahead** in the pending request queue.
* If no pending requests exist further along the current direction of travel, the arm **reverses direction immediately** without visiting the extremity.

## 📚 Core Deep-Dive & Concepts

### 1. The LOOK Scheduling Algorithm

#### Algorithm Mechanics
1. The read/write head moves in the designated direction, servicing requests along its trajectory.
2. The controller continuously checks the request queue: *Are there any pending requests ahead of the current head position in the current direction?*
3. As soon as the **last pending request in that direction is serviced**, the actuator arm reverses direction immediately!
4. The head **never travels to the physical disk boundary** ($0$ or $199$) unless an actual request happens to reside there.

---

#### Total Head Movement Calculation

$$\text{Down Leg: } |53 - 14| = 39\text{ Cylinders}$$

$$\text{Up Leg: } |183 - 14| = 169\text{ Cylinders}$$

$$\mathbf{\text{Total Head Movement (LOOK)} = 39 + 169 = 208\text{ Cylinders}}$$

:::tip Mechanical Distance Comparison: SCAN vs LOOK
* **SCAN**: Traveled from $53$ all the way to the physical boundary at $0$ ($53$ cylinders).
* **LOOK**: Traveled from $53$ only as far as the lowest pending request at $14$ ($39$ cylinders), saving $14$ cylinders on the downward pass and $14$ cylinders on the upward return—an immediate $28$-cylinder reduction in mechanical wear and seek latency!
:::

---

### 3. Circular-LOOK (C-LOOK) Scheduling

#### Algorithm Mechanics
1. The head services requests monotonically in one direction (e.g. towards higher tracks).
2. It proceeds **only as far as the highest requested cylinder** in that direction ($183$). It does **not** proceed to the physical edge ($199$).
3. Once the highest requested cylinder is serviced, the head executes an express return trip **directly to the lowest requested cylinder** ($14$). It does **not** return to Cylinder $0$.
4. At the lowest requested cylinder, it resumes servicing requests in the original forward direction.

---

:::tip Actuator Travel Savings: C-SCAN vs C-LOOK
* **C-SCAN Total Movement**: $382$ Cylinders (swept to physical limits $199$ and $0$).
* **C-LOOK Total Movement**: $322$ Cylinders (bounded strictly by outer requests $183$ and $14$).
* **Mechanical Efficiency**: Immediate reduction of **$60$ cylinders ($15.7\%$ less actuator movement)** with zero starvation risk!
:::

### 5. The Master 6-Algorithm Comparative Benchmark

Here is the definitive comparative summary of all six fundamental disk scheduling algorithms evaluated on the identical standard benchmark:

$$\text{Initial Position: } 53, \quad \text{Direction: Toward 0}, \quad \text{Queue: } [98, 183, 37, 122, 14, 124, 65, 67]$$

| Algorithm | Total Head Movement (Cylinders) | Starvation Risk | Boundary Traversal to $0$ or $199$? | Performance Ranking |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **First-Come, First-Served (FCFS)** | $\mathbf{640}$ | **Zero ($0\%$)** | No | 6th (Worst) |
| **Circular-SCAN (C-SCAN)** | $\mathbf{382}$ | **Zero ($0\%$)** | Yes (Visits both $199$ & $0$) | 5th |
| **Circular-LOOK (C-LOOK)** | $\mathbf{322}$ | **Zero ($0\%$)** | **No** (Bypasses empty ends) | 4th |
| **SCAN (Elevator)** | $\mathbf{236}$ | **Zero ($0\%$)** | Yes (Visits Cylinder $0$) | 2nd (Tie) |
| **Shortest Seek Time First (SSTF)** | $\mathbf{236}$ | **Severe Risk** | No | 2nd (Tie, Unstable) |
| **LOOK (Optimized Elevator)** | $\mathbf{208}$ | **Zero ($0\%$)** | **No** (Reverses at request $14$) | **1st (Winner!)** |

> **Key Architectural Takeaway:**  
> **LOOK achieves the absolute lowest total head movement ($208$ cylinders)** while guaranteeing **zero starvation**, making it the premier real-world scheduling discipline for magnetic disk storage!

### Modern Enterprise Storage: NCQ (Native Command Queuing) in SATA / SAS

Modern enterprise storage offloads disk scheduling from the OS CPU kernel directly into on-disk DSP microcontrollers:

1. **Why Hardware NCQ Outperforms OS Schedulers**:
   - The OS only knows the **cylinder number**, not the exact instantaneous angular position of the spinning platter.
   - The hard drive's on-board firmware knows the exact microsecond rotational location of the heads.
2. **Rotational Position Sensing (RPS)**:
   - NCQ evaluates both **seek time AND rotational latency** simultaneously. If cylinder 98 is 10 tracks away but sector 0 is about to spin under the head, the controller services cylinder 98 before cylinder 65!

## 🎯 Exam & Interview Pitfall Check

:::tip Core Conceptual Questions

**Question 1:** What is the fundamental difference between the SCAN algorithm and the LOOK algorithm?
**Answer:**
* **SCAN**: The actuator arm travels all the way to the **physical extremity of the disk (Cylinder $0$ or Cylinder $Max$)** before reversing direction, regardless of where the last pending request is located.
* **LOOK**: The actuator arm travels **only as far as the final pending request in the current direction**, verifies no further requests exist ahead, and reverses immediately without ever visiting the unrequested physical extremity.

---

**Question 2:** Trace the LOOK algorithm on a disk with 200 tracks ($0\text{–}199$), starting at track $50$, moving **upward** towards higher tracks, with pending requests:
$$\mathbf{82, 170, 43, 140, 24, 16, 190}$$
Calculate the total seek distance.
**Answer:**
1. **Initial State**: Head at $50$, moving upward.
2. **Upward Sweep**:
   - Pending higher requests: `82, 140, 170, 190`.
   - Head services: $50 \to 82 \to 140 \to 170 \to \mathbf{190}$.
   - At $190$, look ahead reveals no requests $> 190$. (Does NOT visit $199$!).
   - Upward distance: $|190 - 50| = \mathbf{140\text{ Cylinders}}$.
3. **Downward Sweep**:
   - Reverses at $190$ and travels downward:
   - Services remaining requests: $43 \to 24 \to \mathbf{16}$.
   - At $16$, look ahead reveals no requests $< 16$. (Does NOT visit $0$!).
   - Downward distance: $|190 - 16| = \mathbf{174\text{ Cylinders}}$.
4. **Total Head Movement**:
   $$\text{Total Seek Distance} = 140 + 174 = \mathbf{314\text{ Cylinders}}$$

:::

:::warning Common Interview Traps

* **The "Visiting Boundary" Trap in LOOK/C-LOOK**: Remember: **LOOK and C-LOOK NEVER visit Cylinder $0$ or $199$** unless the request queue explicitly contains $0$ or $199$! If you include $0$ or $199$ in a LOOK trace, your answer will be marked incorrect.
* **The Direction Reversal Calculation Shortcut**: Because LOOK sweeps monotonically in each direction, you do not need to add every single individual step:
  $$\text{Total Distance} = (\text{Max Requested} - \text{Head}) + (\text{Max Requested} - \text{Min Requested})$$
  (when starting upward). This formula yields the exact answer in seconds.
:::
